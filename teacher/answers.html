what is 3d printing

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. 3D printing is mostly…
    Answer: adding material in layers
    Additive means the part grows from nothing, layer by layer.
  2. 2. Which one is subtractive?
    Answer: whittling a stamp from an eraser
    Whittling removes material. That is subtractive.
  3. 3. A true space story is…
    Answer: astronauts printed tools on the space station
    The file traveled as data. The printer on the station made the object.
  4. 4. Your school printer can reliably…
    Answer: print a small plastic tag or clip
    Desktop FDM is great at small plastic parts. Houses need giant concrete printers and a full building crew.

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. Chuck Hull’s 1984 patent is for…
    Answer: stereolithography (SLA), curing resin with light
    SLA came first commercially. FDM, the melted-filament process in most school printers, is Scott Crump’s later invention.
  2. 2. Why did cheap desktop printers spread after about 2009?
    Answer: key FDM patents expired and open projects like RepRap shared designs
    The machines got easier to copy when core patents lapsed and the RepRap community published plans.
  3. 3. A careful claim about medicine is…
    Answer: printed anatomical models and some surgical guides are real; printed organs are still research
    Models and guides are in use. Printing living replacement organs is not a classroom result.
  4. 4. Strength of an FDM part depends on…
    Answer: how well the layers weld, so direction matters
    Layers can split. A clip printed on its side can be stronger than the same clip printed standing up.

how a printer works

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. The nozzle is…
    Answer: the hot tip where plastic comes out
    The nozzle is the small hot tip. The spool is the filament supply.
  2. 2. The extruder…
    Answer: pushes the filament forward
    Pushing is the extruder’s job. The bed is the table.
  3. 3. A part gets taller because…
    Answer: each new layer is drawn on top of the last one
    FDM is layer by layer. Nothing appears fully formed.
  4. 4. While it is printing, the nozzle is…
    Answer: hot enough to burn skin
    PLA nozzles are often near 200°C. That is far hotter than cocoa.

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. Which part melts the plastic?
    Answer: the hotend
    The extruder only drives the filament. Melting happens in the hotend.
  2. 2. A 0.20 mm layer height means…
    Answer: each new slice is about 0.20 mm thick
    Layer height is the vertical step. Nozzle diameter is the width of the hole, often 0.4 mm.
  3. 3. Why do layers stick?
    Answer: the new melt welds to the plastic underneath before it cools
    FDM bonds by heat and pressure, not by a separate glue in the filament.
  4. 4. The gantry is…
    Answer: the motion system that carries the nozzle
    Gantry means the moving frame: belts, rails, or screws in X, Y, and Z.

safety

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. The nozzle while printing is…
    Answer: hot enough to burn
    Do not touch the nozzle, hotend, or a hot bed.
  2. 2. A water bottle belongs…
    Answer: away from the machine
    Drinks spill into electronics and onto hot parts. Eat somewhere else.
  3. 3. Who removes a stuck print with a scraper?
    Answer: a teacher, or you only if they are coaching you
    Scrapers cut. The point faces away from hands and bodies.
  4. 4. If you smell hot plastic, you should…
    Answer: step back and tell the teacher
    Distance is part of the protection. Do not hover.

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. Why is PLA the school default instead of ABS?
    Answer: ABS needs more heat, warps more, and gives off more troublesome fumes such as styrene
    PLA is easier and lower temperature. It still needs ventilation, and school prints are not food-safe.
  2. 2. A dust mask from art class…
    Answer: is not the main control. Ventilation and distance matter more
    School guidance treats enclosure, exhaust, and not hovering as the real controls.
  3. 3. An overnight print with the air system off is…
    Answer: a bad plan. Printers are not left unattended
    Heaters can fail. Someone responsible has to be able to stop the machine, and the room still needs air movement.
  4. 4. Lowest useful nozzle temperature…
    Answer: usually means fewer emissions, if the print still sticks
    Hotter often means more particles and gases. Stay inside the filament label.

materials

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. Our school filament is…
    Answer: PLA
    PLA is the default. ABS stays off the class printer.
  2. 2. TPU is the one that…
    Answer: can bend like rubber
    TPU is flexible. A name tag wants stiff PLA.
  3. 3. A PLA tag in a hot car may…
    Answer: soften and warp
    PLA’s glass transition is near 60°C. A closed car gets hotter than that.
  4. 4. School prints and snacks…
    Answer: do not mix. Do not print cups or candy tools
    Grooves hold bacteria, and “plant-based” is not a food-safety stamp.

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. PETG compared with PLA is usually…
    Answer: tougher, a little harder to print, and more likely to string
    PETG is a functional step up. It still needs ventilation, and the spool label sets the temperature.
  2. 2. A spool that feels brittle and prints with pops may be…
    Answer: damp. Dry it the way the teacher’s guide says
    PETG and TPU absorb water. Drying is a teacher-run step, not a student experiment with an oven.
  3. 3. The gram estimate on this site uses…
    Answer: about 1.24 g/cm³ for PLA, then an infill model
    PLA is a bit denser than water. The slicer is still the real measurement.
  4. 4. ABS in this room…
    Answer: is avoided. Heat, warp, and styrene fumes do not fit a class default
    ABS can be the right industrial choice in a ventilated enclosure. It is the wrong default beside students.

cad design

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. The best first CAD tool in this course is…
    Answer: Tinkercad
    Tinkercad is free, browser-based, and built for beginners.
  2. 2. A hole in Tinkercad is…
    Answer: a shape set to hole, then grouped
    Holes are negative solids. Group them with the part.
  3. 3. The slicer wants…
    Answer: a 3D file such as STL
    Export STL or 3MF. A picture has no thickness.
  4. 4. BlocksCAD is useful if you like…
    Answer: Snap-together code blocks
    BlocksCAD connects block coding to 3D shapes.

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. Parametric means…
    Answer: important sizes are named numbers you can change
    Change the clearance parameter and the hole should update.
  2. 2. A sensible next tool after Tinkercad is…
    Answer: Fusion education or Onshape Education
    Both are free for students and handle assemblies. FreeCAD is the open-source alternative.
  3. 3. Design units for these projects…
    Answer: millimeters
    Printers and VEX drawings in this course are in millimeters, with inches only when the official VEX note uses them.
  4. 4. Before you export, you should…
    Answer: make sure it is a solid, in millimeters, with the flat face you want on the bed
    The slicer prints the solid you exported, including tiny gaps you forgot to join.

model libraries

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. Before you print someone else’s file you…
    Answer: read the license and write their name
    The license is the permission. Credit is the thank-you.
  2. 2. CC BY-NC means…
    Answer: no selling. Class and gifts can still be okay
    NC means noncommercial. A school fair sale is commercial.
  3. 3. CC BY-ND means…
    Answer: do not change the model
    ND is no derivatives. Print it as the maker shared it.
  4. 4. Printables and Thingiverse are…
    Answer: libraries of files other people designed
    Each file has its own license. The website is not a blanket “yes.”

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. Copyright on a model…
    Answer: starts when the maker creates it, even with no © symbol
    Copyright and patents are different. A design file is copyrighted automatically.
  2. 2. CC BY-SA on a remix means…
    Answer: your new file keeps the same ShareAlike license, with credit
    ShareAlike passes the same freedoms forward.
  3. 3. CC0 means…
    Answer: the maker dedicated the work to the public domain as far as they can
    CC0 is the most open mark. Credit is still good practice, and other laws can still apply.
  4. 4. A NASA 3D model…
    Answer: still needs the terms on that item’s page
    Read the item. Do not invent a license from the agency name.

slicing

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. A slicer…
    Answer: turns the model into layers and printer instructions
    The slicer is software. The printer does the heating.
  2. 2. Infill is…
    Answer: the pattern inside the part
    Infill is hidden structure. 15% is plenty for many tags.
  3. 3. Supports are for…
    Answer: steep parts that would otherwise print in the air
    A flat tag on its back usually needs no supports.
  4. 4. This page’s calculator…
    Answer: is a classroom estimate
    Use it to see the trend. Use the slicer for the real job.

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. Dropping layer height from 0.28 to 0.12 mm usually…
    Answer: adds layers, time, and surface detail
    Height changes the count of layers more than the volume. Time goes up.
  2. 2. G-code is…
    Answer: the instruction file the printer follows
    The slicer writes G-code. You rarely edit it by hand in this class.
  3. 3. For many clips, strength comes first from…
    Answer: more perimeters and a smart print direction
    Walls carry a lot of the load. 100% infill is slow and can warp.
  4. 4. The 12 mm³/s number in the estimator…
    Answer: is a baseline so the class can compare settings
    Real flow depends on the nozzle, material, and speed limits.

troubleshooting

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. Cobweb hairs are called…
    Answer: stringing
    Stringing is stray plastic between parts.
  2. 2. A corner that curls up is…
    Answer: warping
    Warping lifts the part off the bed. A brim is the fix, not the curl.
  3. 3. Spaghetti usually means…
    Answer: the nozzle printed without the part staying put
    Once the part moves, the rest is a tangle.
  4. 4. Your first move is…
    Answer: stop and get a teacher
    Hot parts and moving belts come first. Then the diagnosis.

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. A good test changes…
    Answer: one variable
    If you change four things, you will not know which one helped.
  2. 2. Damp PETG often shows up as…
    Answer: stringing, popping, or weak layers
    Water in the filament flashes to steam in the hotend.
  3. 3. A brim helps warping because it…
    Answer: adds stuck-down area around the part
    More first-layer area, especially at corners, fights lift.
  4. 4. Elephant’s foot (a squashed bottom edge) often means…
    Answer: the first layer is too close or the bed is too hot
    Too far looks like separate lines. Too close smooshes plastic outward.

vex robotics

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. VEX IQ hole pitch is…
    Answer: 12.70 mm, which is 0.5 inch
    VEX IQ uses a half-inch grid, written 12.70 mm on the official drawing.
  2. 2. The official IQ snap hole is…
    Answer: 4.20 mm across
    #8-32 belongs to V5 metal. IQ snap holes are 4.20 mm.
  3. 3. You should 3D print official-style pins…
    Answer: no. VEX says snap geometry breaks. Use real pins
    Print plates with holes. Keep the factory pins.
  4. 4. A mount you printed today…
    Answer: stays on the practice bot unless the current manual allows it
    Fit is not legality. Check the game manual and the Q&A.

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. V5 metal holes are…
    Answer: 0.182 inch square on a 0.500 inch grid
    4.20 mm is the IQ snap. V5 structure pages list 0.182 inch square holes.
  2. 2. The 2026–27 manuals reviewed for this course…
    Answer: prohibit 3D printed robot parts, including non-functional decorations, with narrow exceptions for some tools and controller attachments
    Those two manuals are explicit. A future update or Q&A can change the event you attend. Read that one.
  3. 3. 36 teeth driven by 12 teeth is a ratio of…
    Answer: 3 to 1. The big gear turns once per three crank turns
    Ratio here is driven divided by driver. The arm side is slower and can pull harder.
  4. 4. Strength for a practice mount comes from…
    Answer: more walls, sensible infill, and printing so layers are not the split plane
    Orientation and walls matter. PETG can help, with a teacher’s profile.

business

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. Licensing means…
    Answer: a company makes the product and may pay the inventor a slice
    Licensing lets someone else manufacture. The deal is for adults to sign.
  2. 2. “Patent pending” means…
    Answer: someone filed an application. It might not be granted
    Do not write it on a project unless an application really exists.
  3. 3. A school fair sale should…
    Answer: happen with a teacher or parent, and only if you own the design
    NC licenses and other people’s copyright block a sale.
  4. 4. Copyright is…
    Answer: protection for your drawing, photo, and design file
    Copyright does not own the general idea of “a hook.”

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. Profit is…
    Answer: money taken in minus filament, time, packaging, fees, and failures
    Revenue is the sticker times quantity. Profit is what is left.
  2. 2. A trademark protects…
    Answer: a name or logo that tells buyers the product is yours
    How it works is patent territory, and most student products never need one.
  3. 3. An NDA…
    Answer: is a secrecy promise an adult should read before anyone signs
    Do not sign, and do not ask a company to sign, without a parent or the school.
  4. 4. A royalty is…
    Answer: whatever the contract says, often a percent or an amount per item
    There is no universal school royalty. Adults negotiate it. Sometimes the answer is no deal.

capstone

Quick check · grades 5–6

  1. 1. The take-home bot includes…
    Answer: a pen cup, a swivel head, arms, feet, and a name plate
    It is a multi-part assembly you personalize.
  2. 2. 0.2 to 0.3 mm clearance means…
    Answer: the hole is a little bigger than the pin so it can assemble
    FDM holes shrink. A planned gap, then a test, is the method.
  3. 3. The checklist ticks are saved…
    Answer: in this browser, for this track
    localStorage keeps your build list on this device.
  4. 4. Before you call it done you…
    Answer: test the pen, the stand, and the swivel, then change one thing
    The test-and-improve step is part of the grade.

Quick check · grades 7–8

  1. 1. The starter gear ratio is…
    Answer: 3, because 36 ÷ 12 = 3
    Add the teeth only if you are computing center distance with the module, not the ratio.
  2. 2. A legal launch object in this lab is…
    Answer: a pom-pom or soft foam ball, aimed at a box
    Hard objects are out. People are never the target.
  3. 3. If the gears lock, you…
    Answer: change one thing, such as backlash, and reprint the test
    Forcing a PLA tooth breaks it. The OpenSCAD backlash number is there to remix.
  4. 4. The certificate belongs to…
    Answer: the student who designed, printed, tested, and can explain the ratio
    The celebration follows the checklist. The learning is the build.
