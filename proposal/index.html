What students will be able to do

  1. Explain how an FDM printer builds an object layer by layer, and name its main parts.
  2. Follow a safety contract, run the pre-print and post-print checklist, and spot hazards in the room.
  3. Choose a filament for a job and say why PLA is the school filament.
  4. Design a part in free CAD (sketch, extrude, export) and check its measurements.
  5. Find a free model, read its license, and give credit.
  6. Slice a model, estimate time and grams, and compare two settings.
  7. Name a failed print and fix it by changing one variable.
  8. Design a practice part to the VEX grid and explain gear ratios using the catapult.
  9. Write a product pitch and a one-page business plan.
  10. Build, assemble, test and improve a take-home project, and present it.

Assessment is by the 1-to-4 rubric, the portfolio, and the take-home project. Students can earn a printable certificate and a Printer Operator badge checklist; a teacher still approves every print job.

Safety and supervision

  • PLA only, the school filament in the course. Filaments that need special ventilation are not used.
  • The recommended printers have an open frame, so the nozzle and the 80 degree bed can be reached. The course makes that the first thing students learn: look, do not touch, and wait for the cooldown.
  • A signed safety contract, a printer checklist before and after every job, and a room check are in the teacher kit.
  • Safety glasses for a class set, a HEPA and carbon air purifier in the room, and a plan for good ventilation.
  • A teacher approves every job in the print queue. I propose that printers stay off and cool when the room is empty.
  • I will follow whatever the school requires of instructors, such as background screening, sign-in and insurance paperwork, and I suggest a school staff member is in the room whenever students are present.
