Careers spotlight

People who print for a living

These cards say what the job prints. They do not invent counts or prices. Read the linked pages when you want the details. School PLA prints are practice, not medical devices.

Prosthetic designer

Medical and assistive teams print hands, sockets, and models that match one person. Volunteers in networks such as e-NABLE share open hand designs.

e-NABLE on Grokipedia
Prosthesis on Grokipedia

Aerospace parts designer

Aerospace teams print lightweight brackets, ducts, and tools for aircraft and spacecraft. Some flight hardware is printed in metal, not school PLA.

Applications of 3D printing on Grokipedia
Stratasys on Grokipedia

Vehicle prototype maker

Automotive teams print test parts, brackets, and molds. A fast resin process called CLIP is used for some interior and custom parts.

Applications of 3D printing on Grokipedia

Dental lab technician

Dental labs print models, guides, aligners, and denture pieces from scans of a real mouth. Those resins are not the PLA spool in class.

Applications of 3D printing on Grokipedia
CLIP on Grokipedia

Building printer operator

Some builders print concrete walls or print small models of a building before anyone digs. A house still needs doors, wires, and a roof crew.

Applications of 3D printing on Grokipedia

Footwear designer

Shoe teams have printed lattice midsoles, including Adidas Futurecraft 4D soles made with a CLIP-based process, so a cushion can be a designed pattern.

CLIP on Grokipedia

Tissue researcher

Bioprinting lays down gels that hold living cells. Labs print scaffolds and small tissue models. Whole organs you can transplant are still a research problem, not a classroom result.

Applications of 3D printing on Grokipedia

Robotics engineer

Robot teams print mounts, guides, and grippers that fit one robot. In VEX, a practice print is not automatically legal on a competition robot.
